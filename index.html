Why Big Ears

Most of us learn music as shapes and letters. Good ears hear something else: relationships. Every note in a song has a job, depending on where "home" is.

This app teaches that one idea until it's automatic: hear a note, know its number, play it. That's what lets you work out songs by ear, improvise what you hear in your head, and know where you are on the neck.

The tonal map

Every key has the same seven notes, numbered from its home note, 1:

1W2W3H4W5W6W7H1′

W is a whole step (two frets), H a half step (one fret). The two half steps, 3–4 and 7–1, are where notes lean hardest: 7 pulls up to 1, 4 falls to 3.

Change the key and the letters change, but the numbers, the steps and the feelings stay the same. Learn them once, use them in every key.

The five notes between the seven (♭3, ♯4, ♭7…) are outside notes: colour and tension.

The seven worlds

The map never moves. A world is simply which number feels like home. Same seven notes, measured from the same 1; only the resting place changes, and with it the mood.

  • 1Major · home on 1 · 1 2 3 4 5 6 7 1′ · bright, settled
  • 6Minor · home on 6 · 6 7 1 2 3 4 5 6 · darker; the same notes as major
  • 2Dorian · home on 2 · minor, but with a hopeful 7 (the 1)
  • 5Mixolydian · home on 5 · major with a bluesy flat 7 (the 4); rock and folk
  • 4Lydian · home on 4 · major with a floating, raised 4 (the 7)
  • 3Phrygian · home on 3 · dark, with a half step right above home (the 4)
  • 7Locrian · home on 7 · unstable; rarely home for long

So in minor you still call the notes 6, 7, 1… not 1, 2, ♭3. One map for everything; you just hear a different home.

How to use it

1. Start with Today. It runs a short session (5–45 minutes, your choice) and switches games for you.

2. In every game, sing before you answer. The singing is the training; the tap just checks it.

3. Answer any way: tap the circle, the map, the neck or keys, or play or sing it with the mic on.

4. Earn rings, book gigs. 7 of 10 right earns a ring and opens the next set of notes. Go slowly; speed comes on its own.

Every game has a ? button: how it works, its options, and a guided round.

Numbers around the circle are a fifth apart, so neighbours sound related: 1 sits between 4 and 5, and the seven notes of the key make one unbroken arc. Chords show up as shapes: a major chord is always the same triangle.

    Which numbers make which chord

    Every chord is built the same way: start on a number, skip one, take the next, skip one, take the next. Tap a chord to hear it and see it on the map and neck.

    W whole step (2 frets) · H half step (1 fret) big step: 4 frets on one string small step: 3 frets

    major 1, 4, 5: big step, then small. Bright, stable.

    minor 2, 3, 6: small step, then big. Darker.

    diminished 7: two small steps. Tense; it's the 5 chord without its root (5 7 2 4), so it pulls to 1.

    Options and tips
    Chords

    Outside chords adds the major 3 and 2 chords and ♭7, in patterns where they resolve the way songs use them. Jazz 7ths uses four-note chords in classic patterns like 2m7 – 5(7) – 1maj7; length is set by the pattern. Listen to the bass. Home (1, or 6m in minor) feels like rest; 5 leans toward home; 4 feels like stepping away; 6m is the dark cousin of 1. With only 1 4 5 6m, once you know three chords the fourth is often the one not used yet.
